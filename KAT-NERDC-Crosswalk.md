## 1. The pathway, and why it's your compliance hook

The NERDC computing strand runs across the **entire** primary-and-secondary journey, under three subject names:

| Level | NERDC subject | Status in the curriculum |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary 1–3** | **Basic Science → Digital (ICT) strand** | *Embedded* in Basic Science (not a standalone subject), a short 3-lesson ICT unit per class |
| **Primary 4–6** | **Basic Digital Literacy** | A named core subject (one of 11–13) |
| **JSS 1–3** | **Digital Technologies** | A named core subject (one of 12–14) |
| **SSS 1–3** | **Digital Technologies** | **One of the 5 CORE COMPULSORY subjects**, every senior-secondary student must take it |

**The sales line this earns you:** *"Digital Technologies is now a compulsory core subject through SSS, not an elective. We make your school compliant from Primary 1 to SS3, with your own teachers delivering it."* That "compulsory core at SSS" fact is the single most powerful thing on your landing page, and it comes straight from the NERDC offering. (Coverage starts at Primary 1, the ICT strand embedded in Basic Science, §3, even though that P1–3 strand is packaged as a light insert, not a per-seat course.)

Two supporting facts from the document worth using:
- **Assessment is project-based:** the SSS curriculum ends in a *Final-Year Research Project* (hypothesis → data collection → analysis → **project presentation & defense**). Your assessment/CBT and project-review tooling maps directly onto this, lead with it.
- **A trade subject is compulsory**, and one of the six trade options is *"Computer hardware and GSM repairs."* Not your core, but an adjacent module you could offer later.

## 2. How to read this, two strands, and coverage status

Digital Technologies / Basic Digital Literacy is a **broad** subject: it mixes commodity digital-literacy (typing, Word, Excel, browsing, online safety) with scarce coding/robotics. So every block is tagged by **strand**, which tells you where to spend effort:

- 🟩 **CODING strand** (block coding, Scratch, Python, robotics, web), your IP and edge. **Own it deeply:** full platform, live progress, auto-marking.
- 🟦 **DIGITAL-LITERACY strand** (devices, files, Word/PowerPoint/Excel, internet, safety), commodity any teacher can run. **Cover it lightly:** slides + worksheets + teacher script, or curate free resources under KAT branding. No heavy platform.

And by coverage status:
- ✅ **Have**, existing KAT assets cover it (Scratch, Python, projects, assessment).
- 🔧 **Adapt**, adjacent material / founder skill; repackage into student-ready lessons.
- 🆕 **Build**, new curriculum to produce (heavy for coding, light for digital-literacy).
- 🔎 **Curate**, wrap existing free content (typing, office, internet-safety) in KAT structure/branding; days, not weeks.

## 3. PRIMARY 1–3 · Basic Science → Digital (ICT) strand, **from your real scheme**

At P1–3, ICT isn't a subject, it's a **3-lesson strand embedded in Basic Science**, isolated in your scheme so a Basic Science teacher can slot it in. Pure foundational digital-literacy, **no coding** (age-appropriate; coding first appears at P5-T3).

|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Parts of a computer · common ICT devices · uses of computers | 🟦 Dig-lit (foundation) | 🔎 Curate + 🆕 light |

**P1–3 verdict, and a product nuance:** because this lives *inside* Basic Science, it's not a standalone licensable course with its own seats. Zero coding, zero platform; pure curate/light-build.

## 7. Build backlog, split by strand (this is the whole point)

The two-strand split turns a scary "build a whole broad subject" into a manageable plan: **build the coding strand deeply; cover the digital-literacy strand lightly or curate it.** Roughly 80% of your effort should go into the ~20% that's coding, the only part that's differentiated and hard.

**🟩 CODING strand, own it deeply (your flagship, platform-delivered):**
1. Package existing block-coding/Scratch assets → P5-T3, P6-T2, **and JSS3-T2** (✅ mostly done, one continuous block-coding spine from Primary 5 to JSS 3).
2. Package Python assets → **SS1-T2/T3 Python** (JSS teaches *visual* coding; Python is SSS) (✅ / 🔧).
3. Adapt founder web-dev/JS → **SS2-T2 HTML/CSS** (🔧, direct fit).
4. Adapt/write **SS2-T3 databases & SQL**, introductory, but new to your library (🔧 / 🆕 light).
5. Adapt PictoBlox + your entrepreneurship experience → **SS3-T2 robotics logic + tech entrepreneurship** (🔧).
6. Adapt assessment/project tooling → every capstone/SBA (P6, JSS3-BECE, SS3-WASSCE/NECO) (🔧).

**🟦 THEORY / DIGITAL-LITERACY strand, cover it lightly / curate (the bulk of the lower years, plus SSS awareness topics):**
7. **P1–3 embedded ICT**, nearly all of **P4, P5-T1/T2, P6-T1**, nearly all of **JSS1 & JSS2** (hardware, OS, maintenance, data, system unit, **networks**, web, e-commerce, cloud) plus **JSS3-T1**, and the **SSS awareness topics** (SS1-T1 digital logic, SS2-T1 networking theory, SS3-T1 cybersecurity/cryptography, SS3-T2 AI concepts, SS2-T3 SDLC): all 🔎 Curate + 🆕-light, slides, worksheets, teacher scripts, free content under KAT branding. **No platform for these.** NERDC pitches the SSS ones as intro/awareness, so keep them light, don't over-build. Days each, not weeks.

**Coverage at a glance, now fully mapped (P1 → SS3):** your genuine deep-build coding is a **continuous, mostly-owned spine**, block coding P5-T3 → P6-T2 → JSS3-T2, then Python SS1, web SS2-T2, SQL SS2-T3, robotics-logic/enterprise SS3-T2, plus the capstones. Everything else across all eleven year-groups is theory/digital-literacy you curate or lightly build (and the SSS "new" topics are pitched as awareness-level, so they stay light). You are strongest exactly where it's hard and examined; the large commodity surface is cheap. That's the ideal shape.
